Creating a UTM naming convention for campaigns

Clear campaign labels make a report easier to understand. This guide explains how to name UTM parameters consistently and why those labels are not proof of a sale.

Understand the labels

UTM parameters are short labels added to URLs so compatible analytics systems can group campaign visits. The three common fields are utm_source, utm_medium, and utm_campaign. Source describes where the link was placed, such as a newsletter or social platform. Medium describes the channel type, such as email, social, or paid_social. Campaign groups related links around a purpose, such as traffic_basics_oct2026. Optional utm_content can distinguish creative variations, and utm_term may describe keyword context in suitable campaigns. You do not need every field for every link. Use the fields that answer your reporting questions, and label them truthfully. Calling paid traffic organic does not make it more valuable; it only makes your data harder to interpret.

Create a controlled vocabulary

Choose lowercase values and one separator style, such as underscores. Write down approved medium values so you do not end up with email, Email, and e-mail as separate report rows. Decide whether a source identifies a platform or a specific newsletter and stick to that rule. A simple spreadsheet can contain destination URL, date, source, medium, campaign, optional content variant, and notes. Include one completed example. Avoid putting names, email addresses, phone numbers, account numbers, or other private information into UTM parameters. Tagged URLs can appear in browser histories, logs, screenshots, and forwarded messages. Consistent naming matters more than creating a complicated code for every small variation. Keep the sheet readable enough for a future collaborator to use without guessing.

Build a link and verify it

Start with a destination you control, such as https://example.org/guide. Add a question mark before the first parameter and join the rest with ampersands. For example: https://example.org/guide?utm_source=weekly_newsletter&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=traffic_basics_oct2026. If the original URL already has a query string, append the first UTM field with an ampersand rather than adding another question mark. Properly encode special characters. Open the link in a browser and verify that it loads the right page. If your website redirects, check whether the labels survive. When tracking is permitted, inspect the analytics report for the test session. Do not assume a link is valid simply because it looks correct. Use a short checklist before publishing a campaign.

Compare placements without confusing variables

Suppose you share one educational guide in a newsletter and on a social account. Both links can use campaign=lead_form_guide_oct2026. The newsletter might use source=october_digest and medium=email; the social post might use source=instagram and medium=social. To compare two newsletter headlines, keep the source, medium, and campaign stable while varying utm_content. This makes the intended comparison visible. Record dates and destination promises in your naming sheet. If the landing page changes substantially during the test, annotate that too. An increase in sessions from one source does not prove that it caused later purchases. A person can discover an article in one place, return another way, and buy much later.

Limitations and affiliate links

UTM parameters do not identify individual readers, prove that a page was read, or verify a commission. Tracking blockers, consent choices, redirects, and differences between analytics systems can leave gaps. Some platforms strip query parameters. Before adding tags directly to an external affiliate URL, check that program's rules; altering the link may break tracking or violate its terms. Prefer tagging links to pages you own, then consult the affiliate platform's actual transaction records for confirmed sales. Do not describe a UTM campaign report as proof of income attribution.

Practical checklist

  1. Choose source and medium names.
  2. Use consistent lowercase spelling.
  3. Name campaigns by purpose.
  4. Keep personal data out of URLs.
  5. Test links and redirects.
  6. Verify commissions in the affiliate program.
